Night-Vision Design System

A reference gallery for interfaces used in darkness — bridge, cockpit, control room, observatory. Luminance is the master lever, not hue. Currently showing Tier B · Red/Amber Operations.

Tier B — Red/amber operations. The operator glances between the screen and a dim physical environment. Warm near-black background, red/amber palette. Amber is the brightest non-alert hue and is used sparingly. Saturated red is reserved for alarms.

Tokens for the active tier

Hierarchy is built by darkening backgrounds, not brightening foregrounds. The brightest pixel on screen is capped. Every value is deliberately dim.

Why no pure #FFF and no pure #000 on active surfaces? White text blooms (halation) and still emits blue-green that night-adapted rods and the melanopic system both see. Pure black on active text/surfaces causes smear and black-crush and makes any bright element pop harder. Pure black is fine only for dead space and unused regions — this page sits on near-black .

Buttons & states

States darken or saturate — never spike. Hover shifts toward the accent ring; active applies brightness(0.85); focus shows an in-palette accent ring (Tab to a control to see it), never a system-blue ring. A luminance jump on press is exactly the flash that destroys 20–30 minutes of dark adaptation.

Status — icon + color, never color alone

OK · Nominal Warning · Check depth Critical · Collision risk
Night palettes compress the usable hue range and green sits near the rods' peak (it glares), so status is always carried by an icon/shape plus the hue — never hue alone. In Tier C there is no green or amber at all: status reads by icon + luminance, with saturated red reserved for alarms.

Accessibility / contrast stance

Text is kept large and heavy so it stays legible at lower contrast (WCAG relaxes ratios for large/bold text). Where key data needs more contrast we darken the background rather than push the foreground past the tier's brightness cap.

Tiers A and B still meet normal dark-UI contrast. Contrast deviations are acceptable only under genuine Tier C scotopic constraints, documented and shipped alongside an accessible day mode via prefers-color-scheme.

Gating an uncontrolled bright surface

[ full-spectrum image / map tile / iframe ]
Bright content hidden

An image, video, <canvas>, Leaflet map tile, or iframe can emit one full-spectrum white frame and wipe out your dark adaptation. It stays covered until you confirm.

Every uncontrolled surface — third-party images/video, canvas, map tiles, iframes, and OS default form controls — must be themed or gated. One white frame undoes 20–30 minutes of adaptation.

Final gate — the verification ritual

Code-level checks are necessary but not sufficient. The definitive test is human: view this page at minimum screen brightness in a fully dark room. Let your eyes adapt for a minute. Nothing should glow, bloom, or draw the eye except a real alert. If a white edge, blue ring, or bright panel jumps out, it is wrong — darken or re-hue it, do not ship it.
